How Pilcrow compares

Pilcrow next to five well-known writing editors: Typora, 1Writer, Ulysses, Obsidian and Bear. Every one of them is good at something. This page is here to help you choose, so it is as plain about what Pilcrow lacks as about what it does well.

Where Pilcrow stands out

  • Your own syntax. Write in Markdown, AsciiDoc, reStructuredText or LaTeX in one editor, or define a syntax profile for markup of your own.
  • Very large files. Open multi-gigabyte documents and keep typing smoothly.
  • Publishing. Export to Word, PDF and HTML with equations, diagrams and charts, and compile a whole manuscript into one document.
  • Tools for serious writing. Column mode, on-device translation, style-guide review, and a built-in AI assistant that you connect yourself.

Where the others cover more

  • Platforms. Pilcrow is for Mac and iPad. There is no iPhone, Windows or Linux version.
  • Linked notes. No tags, wikilinks, backlinks or graph view.
  • Publishing. No EPUB export, and no publishing straight to a blog or site.
  • Extending it. No plugins.
  • Hidden markup. Pilcrow shows your markup, styled, rather than hiding it as you type. A rendered Preview, or Side by Side, is one click away.

Feature by feature

✓ The editor covers it – It does not (this includes Pilcrow) ? We could not confirm this

Reading the notes. A word beside a ✓ says how the editor does it: plugin means through an add-on plugin, paid through a paid service, keywords is Ulysses's version of tags, and grammar means grammar checking rather than review against a style guide.

Choosing. If you want linked notes with tags, wikilinks and a graph, Obsidian and Bear offer that and Pilcrow does not. If you need an iPhone app, EPUB export or plugins, look at the others. If your text is more than Markdown, if your files are very large, or if you publish to Word, PDF and HTML, Pilcrow is built for that.
