The story behind Pilcrow

Writing should not feel like renting access to your own words.

Pilcrow began with my frustration as a writer—and with my determination to build something better for the community that taught, encouraged and sustained me.

I was tired of fighting my writing tools

As a writer, I grew tired of tools that treated the act of writing as an opportunity to lock away essential features, collect personal data or push me into someone else’s idea of how a document should work. Too often, the tools meant to help me write became obstacles themselves: subscriptions for basic editing, proprietary formats, unnecessary complexity, artificial file-size limits and features that worked only if I surrendered my privacy or stayed connected to a service.

I wanted an editor where every essential writing and editing feature was free. Not temporarily free. Not free until a document became too large or I needed one more basic function. Free because everyone deserves a private, capable place to think, write, revise and create.

I also wanted those words to remain safe. A journal entry, unfinished novel, research archive, personal letter or collection of notes can be deeply private. Pilcrow is being built on the belief that your writing belongs to you. Privacy should be part of the foundation, not a premium feature or a promise buried in a policy.

The editor should adapt to the writer

Most writing tools ask us to adapt to them. They support a small collection of formats, impose a particular workflow and decide what structure our writing is allowed to have. Pilcrow takes a different approach. You can use familiar markup languages such as Markdown, AsciiDoc, reStructuredText and LaTeX, but you are not limited to them.

You can create your own markup language—one shaped around the way you think and work—without needing to be a programmer. A teacher might design simple notation for lesson plans. A researcher could create structures for sources, observations and citations. A novelist might define markers for characters, locations, revisions or narrative threads. A screenwriter, journalist, student, historian or world-builder could each create something entirely different.

The tool should adapt to the writer, not the other way around.

Your ideas should not have a size limit

Many editors work well until a document becomes truly large. Then they slow down, freeze or fail to open it at all. I wanted Pilcrow to handle gigabyte-sized files because people should not have to divide their work into arbitrary fragments just to satisfy the limitations of an application.

Whether you are opening a novel, a lifetime of notes, a research corpus, a transcript, a data export or a massive archive, the size of your ideas should not be constrained by the editor holding them.

Every language deserves a serious writing tool

Language should not be a barrier. Pilcrow is intended to support writing across languages, scripts and cultures, because writing is one of humanity’s most universal acts. No language should be treated as an afterthought, and no writer should have to abandon their own language to use a capable editor.

Free features, with a community-shaped future

At its heart, Pilcrow is my attempt to build the writing tool I kept wishing existed: capable without being controlling, flexible without demanding technical expertise and powerful without putting ordinary creative work behind a paywall.

Subscriptions have a different purpose here. They are not meant to hold writing features hostage. Subscribers help sustain Pilcrow and earn a meaningful voice in where it goes next. They can help shape priorities, influence the roadmap and participate in the direction of the project. I want Pilcrow to grow alongside the people who use it—not according to advertising incentives, engagement metrics or the demands of distant investors.

My way of giving something back

Every writer benefits from a larger community: from teachers who encouraged us, developers who built open tools, readers who gave our work meaning and countless people who preserved and shared knowledge before us. I want Pilcrow to contribute to that tradition by making thoughtful, private and dependable writing tools available to everyone.

I do not believe creativity should depend on income. I do not believe privacy should be a luxury. I do not believe writers should lose control of their work simply because they chose the wrong application or stopped paying for a service.

I believe your words should remain yours.

Pilcrow is being built for the frustrated writer, the curious beginner, the meticulous researcher, the compulsive note-taker, the student finding their voice and the experienced author who knows exactly how they want to work. It is for anyone who has ever felt that their tools were getting between them and what they were trying to say.

Most of all, Pilcrow is an invitation: to write freely, to shape the tools you use and to help build a better home for the written word.
